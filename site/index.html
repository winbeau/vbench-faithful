Abstract

Fine-grained video benchmarks rely on scoring implementations that faithfully measure their stated properties. We audit nine VBench dimensions and identify three recurring forms of target–evidence misalignment: Target Substitution, Nuisance Entanglement, and Evidence Collapse. Controlled counterfactual tests examine whether scores respond to changes in the target property and remain stable under irrelevant variation. We develop targeted repairs through semantic target parsing, region isolation, a learned motion readout, and preservation of discriminative information. The audit exposes unintended responses to synonymous wording, object and attribute bindings, background variation, local texture jitter, and spatial direction. It also reveals the limits of text-side repair: recovering ordered relation triples cannot restore direction already discarded by an unsigned geometric backend. This framework connects implementation-level diagnoses to explicit behavioral tests and clarifies which stage of a scoring pipeline requires repair.
